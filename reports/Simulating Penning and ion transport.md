# Missing data, not missing theory, limits Penning simulation

Garfield++/Magboltz model Penning transfer with one fitted probability r_P mainly because of **missing heavy-particle data**, not because of the computing cost. Level-resolved quenching rates and, above all, the *ionising branching ratios* for Ar* + polyatomic quenchers have only been measured for the 4s metastables and four of the ten 4p levels. Nothing is measured for 3d, 5s or higher levels. A gain curve constrains only one number per (p, c), Σ r_i·ν_exc,i. The quantum mechanics that could fill the gap (complex optical potentials for Ar* + C₄H₁₀) has never been attempted, so the data cannot simply be computed either. Garfield++ already contains a full Ar de-excitation model, but Schindler could tune its roughly 15 free parameters only *to* the fitted r_P values and could not predict them. For ions, the state of the art is that the drifting species is known from low-pressure drift-tube spectroscopy: in Ar/iC₄H₁₀ it is a C₄H₉⁺-type hydrocarbon ion, near the Langevin limit. **No hydrocarbon-ion mobility has been measured above about 45 Td.** Garfield++ also has no ion-chemistry or species-change machinery, so practitioners load one mobility table. Often it is the wrong one, Ar⁺/Ar. For MX17 the recommendations are: dry r_P ≈ 0.40 (range 0.35–0.45), fitted jointly with photon feedback; wet r_P ≈ 0.36–0.40; and the ion tail should use a hydrocarbon ion with K₀ ≈ 2.0–2.15 (bracket 1.8–2.25), not Ar⁺. That change shortens the simulated ion transit by about 1.5–1.85×. It explains most of the gap between 340 ns and 140 ns, but probably not all of it, unless the field map's mid-gap field turns out to be wrong.

Throughout, *measured* means a published experimental number, *code* means what the current Garfield++ master source actually does, and *inference* means extrapolation or reasoning on top of those.

## Penning transfer is a branching-ratio problem that gain curves cannot resolve

The physics is well understood qualitatively. Penning transfer covers several channels: collisional Penning ionisation of the quencher by Ar*, associative ionisation, photo-ionisation of the quencher by resonance VUV photons, and Hornbeck–Molnar Ar₂⁺ formation at high pressure. These compete with radiative decay, non-ionising (dissociative) quenching, collisional mixing between Ar levels, and three-body excimer formation. iC₄H₁₀ has an ionisation potential of 10.67 eV, below the Ar 3p⁵4s manifold at 11.55–11.83 eV, so **every Ar excited level, including the metastables, is eligible**. In Ar/CO₂, by contrast, only 3d/5s and higher levels (≥13.85 eV) are eligible ([Şahin et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1088/1748-0221/5/05/P05002)). Collision times with the quencher are about 100 ps at unit concentration, far shorter than the 10–50 ns radiative lifetimes, so collisional quenching dominates at a few percent admixture. The 9.8 eV second-continuum excimer photons cannot ionise any common quencher, so excimers mainly *remove* energy ([Şahin 2010](https://doi.org/10.1088/1748-0221/5/05/P05002)).

What blocks a predictive model is the input list. Schindler's thesis sets out what a microscopic model needs: noble-gas transition rates and line shapes, which are available; quencher photo-absorption and ionisation yields, which are available except for isobutane; Ar–Ar collisional rates, which are partly available; and **level-resolved quenching rates *with ionising branching fractions***, which are sparse. Total quenching constants exist for the 4s levels (a few ×10⁻¹⁰ cm³ s⁻¹) and for four 4p levels. For 3d, 5s and higher levels "no measured rate constants could be found", and the ionising fraction is measured only for Ar metastables + C₂H₂ (0.61–0.74). Every other branching ratio is *estimated* as η^0.4 from the quencher's photo-ionisation yield. Schindler concluded that "a 'first-principle' simulation is, therefore, not possible". He tuned the unknowns by simulated annealing to reproduce Şahin's fitted r values, and noted the solution "is clearly not a unique solution" ([Schindler 2012](https://repository.cern/records/a5nhh-0sg66/files/CERN-THESIS-2012-208.pdf)). The live code still carries the 3d→4p transfer constant as "Parameter to be tuned (order of magnitude guess)" and the Hornbeck–Molnar rate as "This value seems high, to be checked!" ([Garfield++ MediumMagboltz.cc](https://gitlab.cern.ch/garfield/garfieldpp/-/blob/master/Source/MediumMagboltz.cc)).

The quantum mechanics is genuinely hard, but it sits one layer down. Ab-initio Penning dynamics uses complex optical potentials V(R) − iΓ(R)/2. It has only been carried out for atom–atom and atom–diatom systems (He*/Ne* + Ar, H₂, HD) and benchmarked by merged-beam experiments at millikelvin energies. Even there, "the highly excited entrance channel and the limited precision of ab initio potential surfaces remain major obstacles" ([Skomorowski et al. 2016](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1601.00433)). Ar* + iC₄H₁₀ would need Γ(R) on a many-dimensional surface, spin–orbit-resolved entrance channels and non-adiabatic coupling to neutral dissociation, which is the very channel that sets the Penning fraction. I found no attempt. Computation is not the bottleneck: plasma-chemistry codes (ZDPlasKin coupled to BOLSIG+, LoKI-B+C, ThunderBoltz) handle Ar metastable kinetics, Penning ionisation and escape-factor radiation trapping routinely ([ZDPlasKin](https://meetings-archive.aps.org/gec/2008/mwp1/60); [LoKI-B+C](https://nprime.tecnico.ulisboa.pt/psi.com/img/Publications/poster_loki-B+C_GEC2025.pdf); [ThunderBoltz](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07913)). An Ar/iC₄H₁₀ reaction set could be written in a day. Its product branching ratios could not, so a more elaborate framework would inherit the same unknowns.

The deepest reason a single r_P survives is degeneracy. The Penning-corrected Townsend coefficient is α' = α₀(ν_ion + Σ r_i ν_exc,i)/ν_ion, so a gain curve fixes only the excitation-weighted sum. Şahin et al. could not separate 4p from 3d ("we can not separate them in the fits, we assume these states have the same transfer probability"). They also had to fit a gain-scale factor g, which is correlated with r, and a photon-feedback term β ([Şahin 2010](https://doi.org/10.1088/1748-0221/5/05/P05002)). Because r_P is defined *relative to a cross-section set*, it is not portable across versions. Andronic et al. fitted 0.37 for Ar/CO₂ 85:15 with Magboltz 2, where the 2014 Şahin formula gives about 0.52 ([Andronic et al. 2004](https://arxiv.org/abs/physics/0402044)). Inverse-swarm machine learning does not break this degeneracy, because the Penning fraction is heavy-particle chemistry, invisible to electron swarm data ([Stokes et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.05842v2)).

## What Garfield++ actually does with r_P today

Magboltz itself applies no Penning transfer. It outputs level-resolved excitation and ionisation frequencies, with 44 Ar levels since v8.6 plus a lumped "HIGH" term. Garfield++ then offers three modes. All three statements below are **code facts** from the current master ([MediumGas.cc](https://gitlab.cern.ch/garfield/garfieldpp/-/blob/master/Source/MediumGas.cc); [MediumMagboltz.cc](https://gitlab.cern.ch/garfield/garfieldpp/-/blob/master/Source/MediumMagboltz.cc)).

| Mode | What it does | Limitations relevant to a Micromegas |
|---|---|---|
| Gas-table rescaling (`AdjustTownsendCoefficient`) | α' = α₀(ν_ion + Σ r_i ν_exc,i)/ν_ion at every table point. The global r applies to all levels above the *lowest* IP in the mixture. | Gets gain right only if r is right. No timing or spatial information. |
| Microscopic "simplified Penning" | At an eligible excitation, with probability r it creates an electron with energy E_exc − I_min, placed within radius λ (default λ = 0). | **No time delay and no displacement.** The source calls it a "Simplified treatment". |
| `EnableDeexcitation` (+ `EnableRadiationTrapping`) | Explicit Ar level cascade, Voigt-profile photon transport, quencher rate files for CO₂, CH₄, C₂H₆, iC₄H₁₀, C₂H₂, CF₄ | **Ar only.** Mutually exclusive with simple Penning. Tuned in 2012 and not validated since. |

The built-in default `EnablePenningTransfer()` refuses any mixture that does not have exactly two components. For Ar/iC₄H₁₀ it hard-codes **r = 0.40** with the comment "There is only one value for this mixture: c = 0.1, p = 1 atm". At 5% it prints a warning and still returns 0.40 ([MediumGas.cc](https://gitlab.cern.ch/garfield/garfieldpp/-/blob/master/Source/MediumGas.cc)). Since 2018 the defaults have only been *extended*: Xe/TMA in 2018, Ne/N₂ in 2020 and Ne/CO₂ in 2021. I found no new kinetic model, no ternary or wet parameterisation, and no new de-excitation data ([Şahin 2021](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1748-0221/16/03/P03026)).

The inference that matters for Micromegas is this: the on-the-spot, zero-delay model gets the *mean gain* right by construction, but it puts Penning electrons in the wrong place and at the wrong time. A 128 µm gap has an avalanche lasting about 1 ns. Penning delays of 0.1 to a few ns and photon ranges of 10–100 µm are comparable to that, so this geometry is where the approximation is least justified, for timing, avalanche lateral size and Polya width. Penning transfer also narrows the avalanche fluctuations, because it adds ionising "trials" ([Schindler 2012](https://repository.cern/records/a5nhh-0sg66/files/CERN-THESIS-2012-208.pdf)).

## Measured r_P values bracket Ar/iC₄H₁₀ 95/5 at 0.35–0.45

The measurement base for Ar/iC₄H₁₀ is thin. The only 1-atm value is **0.40 ± 0.01 at 10% iso**, from fits to Agrawal proportional-counter gain curves. Iso falls off the alkane trend (CH₄ 0.21, C₂H₆ 0.31, C₃H₈ 0.43), which the authors attribute to its compact shape ([Şahin 2010](https://doi.org/10.1088/1748-0221/5/05/P05002)). Şahin's later fit to Bronić & Grosswendt data at **30 kPa**, fitting β at the same time, gives 0.295, **0.189**, 0.258, 0.188 and 0.147 at 3.3, 5, 10, 20 and 30% iso. The non-monotonic 5% point suggests r and β are not cleanly separated in a quencher-poor mixture ([Şahin 2018](https://acikerisim.uludag.edu.tr/items/0be7e437-5772-4e34-a74a-dedfbd782b6f)). Micromegas-specific Garfield fits for Ar/iso 90/10 range from 0.40–0.50 (64–220 µm gaps, Saclay collaboration) ([Bhattacharya et al. 2016](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1605.02921)) up to 0.80, which still under-predicted gain and was blamed on gap tolerance ([Bhattacharya et al. 2013](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1304.6228)). Those Micromegas numbers are detector tuning constants that absorb gap, transparency, calibration and feedback errors. They are not gas properties.

**Dry r_P (inference).** Ar/C₃H₈ is the closest analogue with a measured concentration dependence. Its Garfield fit drops only 3% between 10% and 5% (0.438 → 0.424), because at 5% the collision time (about 2 ns) is still short compared with the radiation-trapped 4s lifetimes ([MediumGas.cc](https://gitlab.cern.ch/garfield/garfieldpp/-/blob/master/Source/MediumGas.cc)). Scaling Şahin's 10% value therefore gives **r_P(95/5, 1 atm) ≈ 0.38–0.40**. The defensible systematic range is 0.35–0.45. The low 30 kPa values are explained mostly by the 3.4× longer collision times at low pressure. MX17's own campaign already found that sweeping r_P from 0.30 to 0.80 cannot reproduce the measured gain-vs-voltage slope, while a feedback term G/(1 − βG) can. That agrees with Şahin needing β at 3.3–10% iso. The practical rule is to **fix r_P ≈ 0.40, or fit it jointly with β and a gain scale, and never let r_P alone absorb the slope.**

**Wet r_P (inference from sourced rates).** The Ar 4s levels at 11.55–11.83 eV cannot Penning-ionise H₂O (IP 12.62 eV). Water quenches them dissociatively, with Ar(³P₂) + H₂O → Ar + H + OH at **k ≈ 4.8×10⁻¹⁰ cm³ s⁻¹** ([Ar–H₂O kinetic model](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/340031752_A_chemical-kinetic_model_of_DBDs_in_Ar-H_2_O_mixtures)). That is comparable per molecule to iso. With iso at 5% and k_iso bracketed at 3–9×10⁻¹⁰, water takes 0.3–1% of Ar* quenching at 300 ppm, 1–3% at 1000 ppm and 3–9% at 3000 ppm. So **r_P(wet) ≈ 0.40 × (0.91–0.995) ≈ 0.36–0.40**. That is a gain change of order 10–20% at most, matching the ≤10–20% gain-vs-humidity bound measured in MPGDs, including a 128 µm Micromegas, in Ar/CO₂ 90/10 over 0–5000 ppm ([Friberg et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.03012v2)). The wet gas is a ternary, so `EnablePenningTransfer()` without arguments fails. Use `EnablePenningTransfer(r, 0, "ar")`. Restricting r to the Ar component also avoids the global form's application of r to any quencher excitation above 10.67 eV. No r_P fit for any wet Ar/hydrocarbon mixture exists.

A consistency caveat applies to the repo's Stage A work. r_P acts on excitations made by low-energy δ-electrons at zero field (W, Fano, Jesse effect) and on avalanche excitations at 30–40 kV/cm. The level mix differs between the two, with more 4s at low energy. One global r_P fitted to gain can therefore mis-set the primary W, and no published Ar/iso cross-check of gain-fitted r against measured W exists ([Schindler 2012](https://repository.cern/records/a5nhh-0sg66/files/CERN-THESIS-2012-208.pdf)).

## The drifting ion is a C₄H₉⁺-type ion near the Langevin ceiling, and its high-field mobility is unmeasured

**Measured: ion identity.** Coimbra's drift-tube spectroscopy (8 Torr, 10–45 Td) finds a dominant "C₄Hₙ⁺" peak in 98/2 Ar/iC₄H₁₀ with **K₀ ≈ 2.26**. They also see a small earlier "C₃Hₙ⁺?" peak and a slow C₈Hₙ⁺ condensation product (K₀ ≈ 1.81). In the T2K gas Ar/CF₄/iC₄H₁₀ 95/3/2 they measure **K₀ = 2.16 ± 0.02**, against 2.15 predicted from Blanc's law plus Langevin. The reaction scheme they give runs fast (k ≈ 10⁻⁹ cm³ s⁻¹) hydride and fragment reactions "leading to t-C₄H₉⁺", followed by slow (k ≈ 10⁻¹⁴) condensation to C₈ and C₁₂ ([Santos et al. 2018](https://indico.cern.ch/event/709670/contributions/3019642/attachments/1670094/2678865/Ion_Mobility_studies_in_Ar-CF4-iC4H10_mixtures_for_the_LCTPC_Collaboration.pdf); [Cortez et al. 2019](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1748-0221/14/04/P04010)). Analogous mixtures behave the same way: Ar⁺ + CF₄ → CF₃⁺ "prevents the formation of Ar₂⁺", and in Ar/CO₂ the noble ion hands its charge to CO₂ "within a few nanoseconds" ([Kalkan et al. 2015](https://acikerisim.uludag.edu.tr/bitstreams/a45ce400-f4f3-4d2b-9b8c-e7c78bc875a9/download)).

**Inference: timescales at 1 atm.** No Ar⁺ + iC₄H₁₀ rate is tabulated, and the Anicich 1993 evaluation has no entry. A Langevin capture estimate gives 1.37×10⁻⁹ cm³ s⁻¹, consistent with the measured Ar⁺ + C₂H₆ rate of 1.15×10⁻⁹ ([Anicich 1993](https://srd.nist.gov/jpcrdreprint/1.555940.pdf)). That puts the **Ar⁺ lifetime at ≈ 0.6 ns at 5% iso**, during which it drifts about 0.3 µm. Fragments funnel to t-C₄H₉⁺ within about another nanosecond. Condensation to C₈ takes about 80 µs, irrelevant to a 128 µm gap. Water does not take over the ion population: hydrocarbon ions cannot protonate H₂O (proton affinity of isobutene 802 kJ/mol against roughly 691 for water) ([Smith & Radom](https://acs.figshare.com/collections/Heat_of_Formation_of_the_i_tert_i_-Butyl_Radical/3416628)). At most 1–7% of Ar⁺ goes to H₂O⁺ at 300–3000 ppm, and that H₂O⁺ itself charge-transfers to iso. Water does lower low-field mobility through clustering, by 5% in K₀ per 100 ppm in Ne/CO₂ at 1 atm ([Deisting et al. 2016](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1603.07638)). The effect should be weaker at gap fields, where the ion's effective temperature is about 1000–1400 K, but the sign is toward slower ions.

**Why Ar⁺ is the wrong table.** Ar⁺ in Ar is slow because resonant charge exchange acts as a large backward-scattering cross section. Garfield's table gives K₀ = 1.53 at zero field, **1.21 at 124 Td**, 1.16 at 150 Td and 0.56 at 1000 Td, against a polarisation limit of about 2.4 ([Garfield++ IonMobility_Ar+_Ar.txt](https://gitlab.cern.ch/garfield/garfieldpp/-/blob/master/Data/IonMobility_Ar+_Ar.txt)). A hydrocarbon ion has no resonant channel with Ar. Langevin gives K₀ = 2.37 (C₃H₇⁺), 2.23 (t-C₄H₉⁺) and 1.99 (C₈ dimer). Measured analogues in Ar-rich gas sit at 80–100% of Langevin: CF₃⁺ at 2.11 against 2.15, and the CO₂-derived ion at 1.94 in pure Ar at 1 atm ([Deisting et al. 2018](https://tpc_gas.cfnssbu.physics.sunysb.edu/tpc_gas/Papers/1804_10288.pdf)). The decisive gap is that **no C₃H₇⁺ or C₄H₉⁺ mobility in Ar has been measured above about 45 Td**. Theory (Wannier heating, short-range repulsion at 0.1–0.2 eV) suggests the 124–153 Td value lies 0–15% *below* the low-field value, not above it.

**Code fact: what Garfield++ can represent.** There is one ion-mobility table per medium (`m_iMob`), no species change, no charge transfer, no clustering, and no automatic Blanc combination. Ions are drifted with drift lines, not microscopic tracking. The only iso-related table shipped, `IonMobility_C8Hn+_iC4H10.txt`, is a flat 0.65 for C₈ in *pure* isobutane at 8 Torr, valid to 45 Td ([Garfield++ Data](https://gitlab.cern.ch/garfield/garfieldpp/-/tree/master/Data); [MediumGas.cc](https://gitlab.cern.ch/garfield/garfieldpp/-/blob/master/Source/MediumGas.cc)). Codes that track ion identity with full collision physics exist only in the plasma community ([Ristivojević & Petrović 2012](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/0963-0252/21/3/035001)). At 1 atm the conversion Ar⁺ → hydrocarbon takes about 1 ns and condensation takes ≫ 1 µs, so a single species is an excellent approximation for the gap. What matters is that it is the *right* species.

## Correcting the MX17 ion-tail arithmetic: 1.5–1.85× from the species, the rest from elsewhere

**The S3 closeout is wrong on this point.** `design/report/S3_ION_CLOSEOUT_2026-08-09.md` states that Blanc's law puts the iso ion at K₀ ≈ 1.16, "4% slower, not 2× faster". That number reproduces exactly as 1/(0.95/1.21 + 0.05/0.65) = 1.16. It used **Ar⁺'s charge-exchange-limited mobility as the Ar-component mobility of a hydrocarbon ion**, which is the one quantity that cannot transfer between species. Done correctly, start from Coimbra's measured 2.26 in 98/2 and back out the Ar-component K ≈ 2.33–2.38, with K(C₄H₉⁺ in iso) taken between the measured 0.65 and the Langevin 0.91. Re-mixing to 95/5 then gives **K₀ ≈ 2.10–2.16 at low field**, consistent with the measured 2.16 in the T2K ternary. Allowing 0–15% reduction at gap fields gives a working value of **K₀ ≈ 2.0–2.15**, bracketed at 1.8 (partial clustering or wet) and 2.25 (Langevin ceiling). This is an extrapolation in E/N by a factor of 3, and it must be labelled as one. A related code comment in `response/avalanche/mx17_aval_calib.py` says that Ar⁺ "is the dominant ion in Ar/iso after charge transfer" and is "the conservative (faster ion) choice". Both statements are backwards: Ar⁺ is gone within a nanosecond, and it is the *slower* ion.

**Field reconciliation.** The two E/N values in circulation are both correct for what they describe. A uniform gap gives 490 V / 128 µm = 38.3 kV/cm, or 153 Td at 1 atm and 293 K. The repo's 123.8 Td comes from the T6 field map's mid-gap E_z = 31.0 kV/cm at the same density. The gas density is fine. The field map's mid-gap field is about 19% below V/d, and **nobody has yet explained why**: 31 kV/cm × 128 µm accounts for only about 400 V. Possible causes include the map's voltage or gap, mesh-plane definition and potential offsets, and field leakage. This should be checked before any quantitative mobility conclusion. The Ar⁺ baseline is consistent with the map: K₀ = 1.21 at 31 kV/cm gives about 318 ns, close to the simulated 340 ns. Transit times for t = d/(K E) at 293 K, 1 atm:

| K₀ (cm²/Vs) | Ion | Transit at 31.0 kV/cm (field map) | Transit at 38.3 kV/cm (V/d) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.21 / 1.154 | Ar⁺ at 124 / 153 Td | 318 ns | 270 ns |
| 1.8 | hydrocarbon, clustered or wet | 214 ns | 173 ns |
| 2.0 | hydrocarbon, central | 192 ns | 156 ns |
| 2.15 | hydrocarbon, low-field Blanc | 179 ns | 145 ns |
| 2.23 | t-C₄H₉⁺ Langevin ceiling | 173 ns | 140 ns |
| **2.75 / 2.23** | **needed for 140 ns** | **140 ns (above every Langevin limit)** | **140 ns (exactly at the ceiling)** |

The species switch is real and large. Taking the ratio of hydrocarbon to Ar⁺ mobility at the same field, it shortens the transit by **≈1.5–1.85×**, so the simulated 340 ns becomes roughly 185–225 ns. That is short of the **2.4×** needed to reach the 140 ns the waveform fits prefer. In the field-map world, 140 ns needs K₀ ≈ 2.75, which no candidate ion in Ar can reach. Taken at face value, the uniform-field world gives K₀ ≈ 2.23, which a hydrocarbon ion could just reach with no Blanc dilution and no high-field reduction. The fork is clear. If the field map is right, a residual factor of 1.2–1.5 must come from something else. Candidates are the weighting field and induced-current geometry near the mesh, the ion birth distribution (ions born near the mesh holes in the higher field), the shaper model, and the ion fraction f_eff, which the HANDOFF already flags as degenerate with T_ion. If V/d is right and the map is not, the hydrocarbon ion alone nearly closes the gap. Published Micromegas tail durations ("~80–100 ns") are round numbers that imply K above Langevin, so they cannot arbitrate ([Giomataris 1999](https://www.slac.stanford.edu/pubs/icfa/fall99/paper1/paper1.pdf)). No published Micromegas waveform fit of the ion mobility in Ar/iso exists, so MX17's fit is itself new information.

## Conclusion

Better Penning simulation needs measurements, not a bigger code: level-resolved (4p, 3d, 5s) quenching rates *and ionising branching fractions* for Ar* + iC₄H₁₀ and CO₂, plus isobutane VUV photo-absorption. With those in hand, Garfield++'s existing de-excitation model, or a ZDPlasKin-style post-processor on its excitation records, could predict r(p, c), delays and spatial spread. Gain curves alone cannot get there, because they measure one excitation-weighted sum; the degeneracy breaks only with additional observables such as pressure scans, timing and avalanche size. For ions the missing measurement is just as specific. A single drift-tube measurement of the C₄Hₙ⁺ mobility in Ar-rich gas at 100–160 Td would replace MX17's largest extrapolation.

For MX17, the immediate actions are: run r_P = 0.40 dry and about 0.38 wet via `EnablePenningTransfer(r, 0, "ar")`, with ±0.05 as a systematic and β fitted jointly; replace `IonMobility_Ar+_Ar.txt` with a custom flat table at K₀ ≈ 2.05, bracketed 1.8–2.25; retract the S3 "K₀ ≈ 1.16" claim and fix the code comment; and treat the 19% mid-gap field deficit in the T6 map as the next thing to explain. It decides whether the hydrocarbon ion closes the ion-tail discrepancy alone, or whether about a third of it lies in the field, weighting or shaper modelling.
