S3 ion investigation — f_ion and the i_ion template are both defended

Follow-up to HANDOFF_S3_ION_2026-08-09. Generated 2026-08-09 19:53 by design/report/make_s3_ion_report.py.

Neither of the two dials the handoff put under suspicion can explain the rise-time floor, and both corrections go the wrong way.

  • f_ion through the real woven mesh is 0.9056 if psi_readout ran, against the parallel-plate 0.9006 — a shift of +0.0050, toward more slow charge.
  • The i_ion template reproduces to 3.8 % at every quantile under an independent reconstruction. 172-ns-to-half is right.
  • Species/mobility is not a factor-2 lever: the real isobutane/cluster ion is ~4 % slower than the Ar+ the emitter assumes.

So the ion model is not the defect. The single-channel shaper model says the data's 150 ns rise demands an effective f_ion near 0.2–0.3 — three to four times smaller than a split now defended by two independent routes. That is a structural contradiction, not a parameter error, and the next axis is the longitudinal × lateral factorisation, in which the ion is handed the surface kernel's lateral shape frozen at its creation point.

Item 2 — f_ion on the readout electrode, through the mesh

Mesh screening does not move the split. f_ion goes from 0.9006 to 0.9056, a shift of +0.0050 — and the shift is in the wrong direction: the true weighting potential puts slightly more charge on the slow ion, not less.

The worry was well posed. mx17_aval_calib.py loads the realistic woven-mesh map through ComponentGrid for the drift field, but keeps a ComponentConstant with SetWeightingField(0,0,1/gap) for the weighting field — even in the meshfield branch. So the measured f_ion = 0.9006 is the parallel-plate in-gap split evaluated on a meshfield avalanche profile, and had never been checked against a through-mesh readout split.

It turns out the answer was already sitting in solve_fieldmap.py: its u_A unit problem (anode = +V_mesh, wires = 0, drift-top = 0) is exactly the readout weighting problem. psi_readout.py reuses that solve verbatim and takes ψ = u_A / V_mesh.

Why it barely moves, structurally. With the ion absorbed on a grounded wire the electron gets 1 − ψ(z) and the ion gets ψ(z), so the split depends only on ψ at the birth height — 14.9 µm above the ESL, where the weave's harmonics have decayed by exp(−2π·136/67) = e−12.8. Whatever the mesh does near itself cannot reach down there. What the mesh does change is the overall weighting gradient: -6.3289e-03/µm against the parallel-plate -6.6667e-03/µm, i.e. the real weighting field is ~5 % weaker — but that scales electron and ion together and cancels from the ratio.

Ion backflow is real but 0.02 %. T6's funnel_ion_endpoints.json measured 94.8% of ions absorbed on the mesh wires (ψ = 0 exactly) and 5.2% escaping into the drift bulk. An escaped ion sits in Edrift = 333 V/cm at ~5 nm/ns, so it moves ~5 µm per microsecond and is frozen on any DREAM waveform timescale: it carries away its residual ψ = 0.00317, weighted 0.052 → 0.00016 of the charge lost.

Why this number is quotable

  • Gated, not asserted. Away from the weave the transverse average of ψ must be exactly linear in z (Laplace on a periodic cell kills every harmonic but the constant). The fit residual over the amp bulk is 4.8e-08 RMS, and ψ(anode) = 1.000000 against an exact 1. A mesh too coarse to reproduce an exactly-linear function would have failed this.
  • Converged. Refining lc_wire 2.0 → 1.2 moves f_ion by 0.0000 and the bulk slope by 0.01 %.

Item 3 — is the S3 v2 i_ion template real?

The measured template is correct. An independent reconstruction sharing no code with the Garfield run that produced it agrees at every quantile to 3.8 %, with one constant — the calib's own measured tarrival = 6.66 ns. 172-ns-to-half is kinematically right.

The question deserved asking: the schema-1 calib shipped i_elec/i_ion as 2000 zeros and silently NaN'd the LUT. The v2 arrays are populated, but populated is not validated. So the ion current was rebuilt from four inputs that share no code with the emitter: Ez(z) from the T6 production map, Garfield's Ar+ mobility table evaluated at the field the ions actually see, the true ψ(z) from item 2, and the measured birth- and absorption-height distributions.

The thing nobody was looking at

The amplification gap runs at 31018 V/cm = 123.8 Td, where Ar+'s reduced mobility is K0 = 1.212 — not the zero-field 1.530 that ions.py's analytic model uses. The analytic 306 ns rectangle is therefore ~21 % too fast. Between the two ion models it is the analytic one that is wrong, not the measured one, and the measured template being slower than it is a feature.

Species: the ×2 worry is not there

The emitter hardcodes IonMobility_Ar+_Ar.txt for every gas, and in Ar/iC4H10 95/5 charge transfer really does move the charge onto an isobutane / cluster ion. But Blanc's law over the mixture gives K0 = 1.162 against Ar+'s 1.212 — the real ion is a few per cent slower, not twice as fast. (Flagged separately to the avalanche session: for a CF4-bearing gas the same hardcoded file would be much further off.)

Two leads sized, so nobody chases the wrong one

The lateral factorisation is not it (−4 ns)

apply_longitudinal convolves the surface kernel with the longitudinal profile, so the ion is handed the surface kernel's lateral shape frozen at its creation point, while the true Ψn broadens as the ion climbs. ions.py's own docstring flags this, and T10 exists to replace it. It moves the rise the right way — but not nearly far enough:

The reason is a scale mismatch. The ion's image width is at most its own height, 149 µm at the end of transit, against an 800 µm channel pitch: the central channel's share falls only from 1.0000 to 0.977 even at the worst moment. T10 will not close this gap.

The peaking-time register is still live, and it has the leverage

The β scan moved the rise by 4 ns and was read as exonerating the electronics. But β is one of the shaper's two parameters, and it is the weak one. The peaking time was never scanned — it is an assumption, state1 = 0x081FD023 from CosmicTb_MX17.cfg, not archived with the run:

One code step is worth 50–65 ns of rise, against β's 4 ns across its whole range. That is the same order as the 104 ns discrepancy being chased. The toy runs ~55 ns below the full chain, so no code here should be read as "the answer" — but the leverage is real, and it means the electronics axis is not exonerated. It was tested on the parameter that could not have caused the problem.

Caveat that has to be checked before anyone gets excited: the upper rise quantiles currently AGREE between sim and data (p95 608 vs 602 ns), and those are set by track geometry rather than the shaper, so a faster shaper should move the fast side much more than the slow side — which is the shape of the observed defect. That prediction is testable with one Stage B point and has not been tested.

What this does not rule out

  • β, jointly. The β scan moved the rise by 4 ns over 0–0.75, but it was run with f_ion = 0.90. A joint (β, f_ion) fit is not the same experiment.
  • The amplitude deficit. Untouched and deliberately so: the no-ions sim still peaks at ×0.63 of data, so amplitude is upstream of all of this.
  • Anything at 490 V only. Every number here is the pooled 490 V bench point in Ar/iC4H10 95/5. The mobility is field-dependent, so a different mesh voltage moves the transit.
