       Even at the most favourable corner of the resistivity scan, A1 predicts a
       drain three orders of magnitude SLOWER than what is measured.
       to get 6.3 µs at  0.5 MΩ/sq the strips would have to be grounded
         every   16.2 mm — not every 412 mm.
       to get 6.3 µs at  1.0 MΩ/sq the strips would have to be grounded
         every   11.5 mm — not every 412 mm.
       to get 6.3 µs at  2.0 MΩ/sq the strips would have to be grounded
         every    8.1 mm — not every 412 mm.
       to get 6.3 µs at  5.0 MΩ/sq the strips would have to be grounded
         every    5.1 mm — not every 412 mm.

       CONCLUSION: A1 as literally stated is refuted by the data.
       Candidate explanations, in the order worth testing:
         (a) the strips are not the drain path at all — the measured tau_g may be
             the pad-plane/front-end RC rather than sheet transport;
         (b) there is a conductive path at a much finer pitch than the active width
             (a grounding grid, or the paste contacting the bus through vias);
         (c) rho_s is far below the scanned 0.5-5 MΩ/sq.
       Until this is settled, the solver's tau_drain_s is an EXPLICIT INPUT and the
       production scan should carry it as a nuisance parameter pinned to the
       measurement, not derived from A1.
