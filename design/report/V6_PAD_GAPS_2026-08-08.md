# V6 — the 100 µm inter-pad gaps are NOT safe to ground

**Date:** 2026-08-08 · **Code:** `response/solver/v6_pad_gaps.py` ·
**Log:** `design/report/v6_pad_gaps.log` · **JSON:** `design/report/v6_pad_gaps.json`
**Plan rows updated:** §3 geometry model W1/W2, §3 validation V6, T5.

---

## Verdict

**W1 is wrong by 27 %, not by "a few %", and the plan's own estimate
(`RESPONSE_SIM_PLAN.md:545`) is refuted.** Treating the 100 µm channels between
the pads as grounded copper — which they are not; there is no copper there —
removes 24 % of the pad plane's drive. Exposing them raises the prompt image
charge captured by the readout from **0.67003 to 0.85194 (+27.2 %)**.

The second number matters more than the first: W1 also invents a **4.5× swing in
signal amplitude across one pad cell** that the real board does not have.

| | prompt capture | vs W1 | min over the cell | max | max/min |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **W1** gaps grounded at the pad plane | 0.67003 | — | 0.1947 | 0.8783 | **4.51** |
| **W2** nominal (26 µm glue + 264 µm FR4 to L5) | **0.85194** | **+27.2 %** | 0.7558 | 0.8812 | **1.17** |
| W2, FR4 halved (132 µm) | 0.83652 | +24.8 % | 0.6915 | 0.8809 | 1.27 |
| W2, open (no conductor below at all) | 0.86350 | +28.9 % | 0.8032 | 0.8813 | 1.10 |

**The board's internal stackup does not matter.** Across the whole bracket —
ground right at the pad plane through to no conductor below at all — the answer
moves 1.7 %. So `design/NEEDED_INPUTS.md` §6's open FR4 thickness, flagged there
as the dominant board uncertainty, **does not propagate into the response**.
That is a closure, and it is worth as much as the headline number.

## What the number means, in one line

Prompt capture is exactly `S(0)/C(0) × <V_pad-plane>`, so this is a statement
about one average:

- W1 pins the inter-pad channel at 0, giving `<V> = 0.7600` — precisely the
  metal fraction `(680/780)²`. That is where §0a's certified anchor
  `(PAD_SIZE/PAD_PITCH)² × S(0)/C(0)` comes from — 0.665023 on the legacy
  bare-kapton stack, 0.670026 on the production kapton+glue one, which is the
  W1 row above.
- The real channel is 100 µm wide between two pads at V_w, with ~290 µm of
  dielectric to the nearest conductor below and 219 µm to the mesh above. It
  **floats to 0.86 of the pad potential**, giving `<V> = 0.9664`.

This is a **DC error, not a fine-structure error** — which is why it does not
decay with insulator thickness (checked: the shift is +27 % at 50 µm kapton and
still +28 % at 5 mm). The intuition "the pad plane looks uniform from far away,
so the gaps cannot matter" is wrong: what becomes invisible at distance is the
*structure* of the boundary, not its *mean*, and W1 gets the mean wrong.

## Where the 27 % lives

Not in the peak. One pad's prompt kernel, read at pad centres:

| | self | +1 pad | diagonal (the checkerboard d=±1) |
|---|---|---|---|
| W1 | 0.877619 | 1.65e-04 | 1.68e-06 |
| W2 nominal | 0.879310 | 4.32e-04 | 1.19e-05 |

A deposit on a pad centre is unaffected (0.8776 → 0.8793, +0.2 %). The entire
shift comes from the **24 % of deposits that land over a channel**, where W1
says 0.19–0.5 and W2 says 0.76–0.88. Prompt neighbour sharing rises by 2.6×
(edge) and 7× (diagonal), but from a base so small that it stays negligible
against the diffusion-driven sharing that sets c1 (plan §7).

Scaling with the gap width is monotone and vanishes correctly:

| gap | 0 µm | 25 | 50 | **100 (as built)** | 150 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| shift | +0.0 % | +6.5 % | +13.2 % | **+27.2 %** | +41.9 % |

## Why this is believable — four independent tiers, all passing

1. **The two-port reproduces the certified solver.** `stack_2port` is a
   coth/csch cascade with an eliminated interface potential; `wpot.stack_coeffs`
   is a tanh/sech ABCD cascade. They are independently arranged and agree at
   **1.7e-16 (C) and 7.2e-16 (S)**. Passivity `A11 A22 − A12² > 0` holds, which
   is what makes the mixed-BC operator positive definite.
2. **Collapse the gap and W2 *is* W1.** With pitch-sized pads there is no
   exposed boundary, and the closed-form sum rule `S(0)/C(0) = 0.881583` comes
   back at every point of the plane to **1.3e-16** — for all three substrate
   models.
3. **Ground the gap from just below and the CG solve walks back to W1**, first
   order in the standoff: error ×10 for every ×10 in h (9.80, 9.98, 10.00).
   Unlike (2), this actually runs the mixed-BC machinery — operator,
   preconditioner and restriction — so it is the check that would catch a sign
   or a mask error in them.
4. **An independent real-space (x, z) finite-difference solve** — no Fourier,
   no two-port, no M operator, nothing shared. On strip pads (the only geometry
   2-D can reach): W1 agrees to 9.6e-14, W2 to 3.8e-4 (the FD's own edge
   discretisation). The strip case has its own closed form,
   `(680/780) × S(0)/C(0) = 0.768560`, and the solver returns it exactly.

Numerics: the 5 µm cell puts every pad edge exactly on a cell boundary (780 and
680 share a factor 20), so the metal mask is exact. Convergence in cell size is
first order — the metal edge is an r^(−1/2) field singularity — with a
Richardson limit of 0.852998, i.e. the quoted 5 µm number is 0.12 % low, far
inside the effect. The answer is independent of box size from 2×2 to 8×8 pads.

## What this does NOT establish

- **It is the prompt kernel only.** As the ESL relaxes it becomes the dominant
  conductor between the deposit and the pads and screens the pad plane's
  structure, so the late-time sensitivity should be *bounded by* the prompt one
  rather than equal to it. That is an argument, not a number. Turning it into a
  number needs the ESL pattern in the box, which destroys the 780 µm
  periodicity this calculation is built on (800 vs 780 µm beat → back to the
  31.2 mm superperiod) and makes the mode coupling dense. **Not done.**
- **The L5/L6 traces are treated as a solid ground plane.** They are really the
  Y and X bus routing (`response/common/channel_map.py` parses both layers out
  of the gerbers — Ø0.50 mm via rings plus 0.098 mm bus lines, 0.458/0.457
  coverage inside the active area — so a trace mask *is* constructible from what
  the repo already has; it was not used). Two things are therefore idealised:
  the layer is 46 % copper, not solid, and charge reaching it lands on a
  **channel** rather than being lost. Both are bounded by the bracket already
  run: solid ground at L5 through no conductor at all spans 1.7 %, and 46 %
  coverage sits inside it. So this is a ≲2 % refinement on a 27 % effect, not a
  loose end that could overturn it.
- **Nothing here re-solves S1.** Every shipped `greens_comb_*.npz` is still W1
  and still carries the +27 % deficit.

## Consequences, in the order they should be acted on

1. **Every absolute amplitude the chain predicts is ~27 % low**, including the
   ADC-scale row of §0a's certification table. Ratios and sharing fractions are
   almost untouched (the peak kernel moves 0.2 %), so the §9 c1 prediction and
   the z-dependence result survive; the *normalisation* does not.
2. **The sim currently carries a spurious 4.5× sub-pad gain map.** Any study
   that looks at amplitude versus position within a pad cell — efficiency maps,
   the ESL-phase beat, hit-finding thresholds near a pad boundary — is reading
   a W1 artifact. This is testable in data: the real detector should show ~1.2×,
   not 4.5×.
3. **Re-solving S1 under W2 is now the blocker for absolute amplitude.** The
   production solver assumes a full Dirichlet pad plane throughout, and lifting
   that is not a coefficient change — it makes C(k), S(k) into operators that
   couple Bloch families the ESL pattern already couples. **Not attempted here.**

   *Two corrections to what this section said when first written (2026-08-08
   evening, from the sizing exercise in plan next-in-order item 3):*

   **(a) "dense over 3120 modes per k_y" was wrong in both directions.** The gap
   lattice couples k_y as well, so the families are larger (~24 960 at ny = 512)
   — but there are only 64 independent k_y classes, halved again by y-parity,
   and three exact reductions collapse the cost: the eigendecomposition is
   drive-independent (one per family serves all 42 kernels), it serves the whole
   ρ_s grid (M ∝ 1/ρ_s, so eigenvectors are invariant and eigenvalues scale),
   and everything is real-symmetric at the production registration. Brute force
   comes out at ~770 core-h — one overnight condor fan-out — and is the chosen
   route.

   **(b) The effective-diagonal `C_eff(k), S_eff(k)` route suggested here is
   ruled out, and more decisively than "it neglects the off-diagonal".** Any
   diagonal model keeps the form Ψ = IFFT[(S_eff/C_eff)(k)·V̂_pad] with the W1
   Dirichlet drive, so its mean capture is (S_eff/C_eff)(0) × 0.76003. Matching
   W2's 0.85194 needs (S_eff/C_eff)(0) = **1.121**, i.e. a capacitive divider
   ratio greater than one — unphysical. **It cannot reach the right answer even
   on average.** It cannot fix the modulation either: a k = 0 rescale leaves
   max/min *exactly* unchanged at 4.51, and k-dependent smoothing lifts the
   minimum only by collapsing the maximum (200 µm of extra smoothing gives
   max/min 1.30 but drops the peak to 0.759 against W2's 0.881), while the mean
   is invariant throughout at 0.670. The only diagonal scheme that could work
   would drive the gaps at their floated potential — which is guessing the
   answer this solve computes.
