# Ion charge transfer, ion identity and ion mobility in gaseous-detector mixtures, and how simulations treat them

Research notes, compiled 2026-10-15. Primary sources were read directly where possible: the Garfield++ master branch on gitlab.cern.ch, fetched 2026-10-15; three RD51 talks by the Coimbra group (2017, 2018, 2020); Deisting et al. (arXiv 2016, NIM A 2018); and Fisher et al. (1990). Other items come only from abstracts or search snippets, and are flagged as such. Several full-text JINST papers (Cortez 2019 iC4H10, Encarnação 2015 Ar-CO2, Trindade 2014 Ar-CH4) could not be retrieved. Their K0 tables are only partly covered here.

Abbreviations: K0 is the reduced mobility in cm² V⁻¹ s⁻¹, normalised to 273.15 K and 1 atm. 1 Td = 10⁻¹⁷ V cm². At 1 atm and 293 K, 1 Td corresponds to about 0.25 kV/cm, and 124 Td to about 31 kV/cm.

## Q1. Which ions actually drift in Ar/iC4H10 and similar mixtures, and why (charge-transfer chain, rates, branching, clustering, timescales)

### Takeaway
In every noble-gas/quencher mixture studied, the noble-gas ion survives only for nanoseconds or less at 1 atm. It hands its charge to the molecular additive by (dissociative) charge transfer. The ion that drifts is a molecular ion of the quencher, or a cluster or condensation product of it. In Ar/iC4H10 at a few % iC4H10, Coimbra identifies the dominant drifting ion as a "C4Hn+" ion (probably t-C4H9+), with a small "C3Hn+" component and slower C8Hn+ and C12Hn+ condensation products. In Ar/CO2 and Ne/CO2 the drifting ion is CO2+ or CO2+·(CO2)n. In Ar/CH4 it is CH5+, C2H5+ or a 3-carbon ion. In Ar/CF4 it is CF3+.

### Cited Findings

**Ar / iC4H10 (and Ar/CF4/iC4H10, the LCTPC "T2K-like" gas 95/3/2)**
- Coimbra (M.A.G. Santos, A.F.V. Cortez, R. Veenhof, F.I.G.M. Borges, C.A.N. Conde; RD51 meeting, TUM, June 2018) list the appearance energies of iC4H10 ions. They are C3H6+ 10.88 eV, C3H7+ 11.16 eV, t-C4H9+ 11.60 eV, iso-C4H10+ 10.23 eV, C2H5+ 13.80 eV, C2H4+ 14.07 eV and C3H5+ 14.55 eV. The "most produced primary ions" are C3H7+, C3H5+, C2H3+ and C3H6+ — [Santos et al., RD51 June 2018 slides](https://indico.cern.ch/event/709670/contributions/3019642/attachments/1670094/2678865/Ion_Mobility_studies_in_Ar-CF4-iC4H10_mixtures_for_the_LCTPC_Collaboration.pdf)
- The same slides give the reaction scheme. Fast reactions with k ≈ 10⁻⁹ cm³/s run iC4H10 + I+ → CxH2x+C4−xH9−2x+ + HI, or → CxH2x+1I + C4−xH9−2x+ (x = 0–3), "leading to t-C4H9+". Slow condensation reactions with k ≈ 10⁻¹⁴ cm³/s follow: iC4H10 + iC4H10+ → C8H18+ + H2, iC4H10 + t-C4H9+ → C8H17+ + H2, and then on to C12H26+ and C12H25+ — [Santos et al. 2018](https://indico.cern.ch/event/709670/contributions/3019642/attachments/1670094/2678865/Ion_Mobility_studies_in_Ar-CF4-iC4H10_mixtures_for_the_LCTPC_Collaboration.pdf)
- In 98% Ar / 2% iC4H10 at 20 Td and 8 Torr, the spectrum has a dominant C4Hn+ peak (K0 ≈ 2.26), a small early peak labelled "C3Hn+ ?" and a small late C8Hn+ peak (K0 ≈ 1.81, extrapolated). One peak is seen up to 70% Ar, and two peaks from 80% Ar. "Ar+ + iC4H10 → products" is described as a charge-transfer reaction — [Santos et al. 2018](https://indico.cern.ch/event/709670/contributions/3019642/attachments/1670094/2678865/Ion_Mobility_studies_in_Ar-CF4-iC4H10_mixtures_for_the_LCTPC_Collaboration.pdf)
- Ar-CF4-iC4H10 (95-3-2), 8 Torr: "Same peaks as in 98% Ar – 2% iC4H10 mixture". The main peak is C4Hn+ with measured K0 = 2.16 ± 0.02, against a theoretical 2.15. "Increasing pressure leads to C12Hn+ formation!" — [Santos et al. 2018](https://indico.cern.ch/event/709670/contributions/3019642/attachments/1670094/2678865/Ion_Mobility_studies_in_Ar-CF4-iC4H10_mixtures_for_the_LCTPC_Collaboration.pdf)
- In pure iC4H10 (15 Td, 8 Torr) the observed ions are C8Hn+ (K0 ≈ 0.65) and C12Hn+ (K0 ≈ 0.60). The light primary fragments are not seen at the detector — [Santos et al. 2018](https://indico.cern.ch/event/709670/contributions/3019642/attachments/1670094/2678865/Ion_Mobility_studies_in_Ar-CF4-iC4H10_mixtures_for_the_LCTPC_Collaboration.pdf)
- The published paper is A.F.V. Cortez, M.A.G. Santos, R. Veenhof, J. Escada, P.N.B. Neves, F.P. Santos, C.A.N. Conde and F.I.G.M. Borges, "Experimental ion mobility measurements in pure iC4H10 and Ar-iC4H10 mixtures", JINST 14 P04010, published 17 Apr 2019. It covers 8 Torr, 10–45 Td, room temperature and 5–95% Ar. K0 is taken from the peak centroid of the time-of-arrival spectra — [JINST 14 P04010 (2019)](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1748-0221/14/04/P04010). The paper says the C12Hn+ mobility should be treated as an upper bound, because C12Hn+ forms through slow reactions from its C8Hn+ predecessor — [search summary of the same paper](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1748-0221/14/04/P04010)
- An iC4H10 radiolysis/ion-chemistry review states that C3H7+ is the most probable ion in isobutane, produced in about 30% of ionizations — [ResearchGate: Electron impact ionization and ion reactions in n-butane (snippet)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/230927357_Electron_impact_ionization_and_ion_reactions_in_n-butane)

**Ar+ + alkanes: closest available rate and product data**
- Flowing-afterglow measurements of Ar+ with CH4, C2H4, C2H6, C3H6 and C3H8 at thermal energy give total rate constants of (0.78–1.1)×10⁻⁹ cm³ s⁻¹, which is 60–92% of the Langevin value. The main channels are dissociative charge transfer with C–H cleavage for CH4, C2H4, C2H6 and C3H6, but **C–C cleavage for propane**. 85–95% of the available energy goes into internal modes of the parent ion — [Tsuji et al., J. Chem. Phys. 98 (1993), doi:10.1063/1.464234 (abstract)](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.464234)
- Ar+ + CF4 → CF3+ (the only product at thermal energy) has total k = 7.0 (±2.6)×10⁻¹⁰ cm³ s⁻¹ from guided-ion-beam work. Earlier drift-tube work gave 8.0×10⁻¹⁰ and ICR work 6.4×10⁻¹⁰, against a Langevin (LGS) rate of 7.6×10⁻¹⁰ — [Fisher, Weber & Armentrout, J. Chem. Phys. 92, 2296 (1990)](https://tpc_gas.cfnssbu.physics.sunysb.edu/tpc_gas/Papers/Fisher.pdf)
- Coimbra state that in Ar-CF4 the charge transfer Ar+ + CF4 → CF3+ + Ar + F "prevents the formation of Ar2+", and that adding Ar "leads to the same ion as in pure CF4" — [Santos et al. 2018](https://indico.cern.ch/event/709670/contributions/3019642/attachments/1670094/2678865/Ion_Mobility_studies_in_Ar-CF4-iC4H10_mixtures_for_the_LCTPC_Collaboration.pdf)

**Ar / CO2, Ne / CO2**
- In Ar-CO2 or Ne-CO2 at atmospheric pressure, avalanches first produce mainly Ar+, Ne+ and CO2+. The noble-gas ions pass their charge to CO2 "within a few nanoseconds". Shortly afterwards CO2+ picks up neutral CO2 to form clusters, mainly CO2+(CO2)n. Because the cluster ions are slower, ion-induced signals are altered, which the authors expect to matter for Micromegas and drift tubes — [Y. Kalkan, M. Arslandok, A.F.V. Cortez, Y. Kaya, İ. Tapan, R. Veenhof, "Cluster ions in gas-based detectors", JINST 10 P07004 (2015) (abstract via repository)](https://acikerisim.uludag.edu.tr/bitstreams/a45ce400-f4f3-4d2b-9b8c-e7c78bc875a9/download)
- In pure CO2, ionisation at 25 eV gives CO2+ 84.7%, CO+ 3.7% and O+ 5.4%. Symmetric charge transfer CO2+ + CO2 has k = 3.7×10⁻¹⁰ cm³/s. Clustering CO2+ + CO2 + M → CO2+·CO2 + M has k = 2.1×10⁻²⁸ cm⁶/s, which gives τ = 71.6 ns at 8 Torr and **τ = 7.9 ps at 760 Torr**. The measured ion is CO2+·CO2 with K0 ≈ 0.97, against a Langevin estimate of 1.17 — [Cortez, "A practical approach to ion mobility", New Horizons in TPCs, Oct 2020](https://indico.global/event/6159/contributions/50740/attachments/25481/43821/RD51_Presentation_-_Ion_mobility.pdf)
- From Hiraoka et al. (1988) van 't Hoff data, CO2+(CO2)n clusters (n = 1–6) form exothermically. Clusters form even at low pressure, and larger ones at low T and high p — [Cortez 2020](https://indico.global/event/6159/contributions/50740/attachments/25481/43821/RD51_Presentation_-_Ion_mobility.pdf)
- Deisting et al. (ALICE) state that in both Ar-CO2 and Ne-CO2 "the drifting ions … are CO2 ions or larger molecules based on CO2" — [Deisting et al., arXiv:1603.07638 (2016)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1603.07638)

**Ar / CH4**
- Methane ionisation at about 25 eV gives CH4+ 56.5% and CH3+ 40.5%. The fast follow-up reactions are CH4+ + CH4 → CH5+ + CH3, CH3+ + CH4 → C2H5+ + H2, C2H5+ + CH4 → C3H7+ + H2, and similar. "Ar+ + CH4 → products" is a charge transfer. In 93/7 Ar/CH4 the slides note the "same peaks as in pure CH4, different amplitudes", with CH5+ labelled — [M.A.G. Santos et al., RD51 mini-week Dec 2017](https://indico.cern.ch/event/676702/contributions/2809870/attachments/1574392/2486508/MSantos_RD51_Miniweek_December2017.pdf)
- Trindade et al., "Experimental study on ion mobility in Ar-CH4 mixtures", JINST 9 P06003 (2014), worked at 5–8 Torr and 17–43 Td. For 2.5–10% CH4 the peaks are attributed to 1-carbon (CH5+), 2-carbon (C2H4+, C2H5+) and 3-carbon ions — [OSTI record (abstract)](https://www.osti.gov/etdeweb/biblio/22354543)

**Timescales**
- Coimbra's general criterion is a reaction time τ = 1/(k[R]). If τ ≪ t_drift, only the final ion is seen. If τ ≳ t_drift, the mixed predecessor/product population biases the apparent mobility. Example: Xe+ + 2Xe → Xe2+ (k = 2.0×10⁻³¹ cm⁶/s) gives τ = 75 µs at 8 Torr but 8.3 ns at 760 Torr — [Cortez 2020](https://indico.global/event/6159/contributions/50740/attachments/25481/43821/RD51_Presentation_-_Ion_mobility.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Ar+ + iC4H10 timescale at 1 atm.** No measured rate was found (see Gaps), so this is a Langevin estimate. α(iC4H10) ≈ 8.1 Å³ and μ(Ar–iC4H10) ≈ 23.7 u give k_L ≈ 2.34×10⁻⁹·√(α/μ) ≈ 1.4×10⁻⁹ cm³/s. That is consistent with the measured (0.8–1.1)×10⁻⁹ for Ar+ + C3H8 and with Coimbra's "k ≈ 10⁻⁹" figure. At 1 atm with 2% / 5% / 10% iC4H10 ([R] ≈ 4.9×10¹⁷ / 1.2×10¹⁸ / 2.4×10¹⁸ cm⁻³), τ ≈ 1.5 ns / 0.6 ns / 0.3 ns. In a Micromegas, Ar+ therefore converts within the avalanche region, after drifting only about 0.1–1 µm. The Ar+ signal contribution is negligible.
- **Product of Ar+ + iC4H10.** Ar+ carries 15.76 eV against IP(iC4H10) ≈ 10.6 eV, so the transfer is strongly exothermic and dissociative. By analogy with propane (C–C cleavage dominates), the primary products should be C3H7+ (+CH3) and C3H6+/C3H5+, with some t-C4H9+. Hydride/proton-transfer reactions with iC4H10 at ~10⁻⁹ cm³/s then funnel these into t-C4H9+, the thermodynamic sink, within about 1 ns at 1 atm and a few % iC4H10. This matches Coimbra's dominant "C4Hn+" peak with a minor "C3Hn+" one.
- **C8/C12 condensation.** It has k ≈ 10⁻¹⁴ cm³/s, so at 1 atm with 5% iC4H10, τ ≈ 1/(10⁻¹⁴ × 1.2×10¹⁸) ≈ 80 µs. That is long compared with the ~100–200 ns ion transit of a 128 µm Micromegas gap. It is comparable to drift times in a cm-scale TPC drift volume, and at 2% iC4H10 (about 200 µs) longer. For Micromegas ion tails at 1 atm, the relevant ion is C4H9+ (or C3H7+), not C8/C12.
- **CO2 at 1 atm.** τ_cluster ≈ 8 ps, so CO2+·CO2 (or larger clusters) forms essentially instantly at low field. Whether it survives at Micromegas-gap fields is a separate question (see Q3).

### Gaps
- **No measured rate constant or product branching was found for Ar+ + iC4H10** (thermal or energy-resolved), in either the snippets or the fetched pages. The Anicich compilation (JPL Publication 03-19, 2003) and the NIST kinetics database should be checked. Anicich could not be fetched in this session.
- Ar+ + CO2 and Ar+ + CH4 rate constants were not retrieved from a primary source. The Ar/alkane total range (0.78–1.1)×10⁻⁹ includes CH4, but the CO2 value is not given here.
- The full text of Cortez et al. 2019 (JINST 14 P04010) was not retrieved. The exact K0 tables vs % Ar and E/N, and the paper's final species assignments, are only available here from the 2018 preliminary slides.
- There are no Coimbra data at 1 atm. All their ion-identity assignments are at 6–12 Torr, where three-body clustering is about 100× slower.

## Q2. Measured mobilities (Coimbra and others), with numbers, and their E/N ranges

### Takeaway
Low-field K0 values (about 0.8–45 Td) are well measured for the drifting ions in most detector mixtures. Typical values in Ar-rich mixtures are 1.9–2.3 cm²/Vs (C4H9+ in Ar ≈ 2.2–2.3; CO2-based cluster in Ar ≈ 1.9). Mixtures follow Blanc's law within errors. All Coimbra data stop at ≤ 45 Td and about 8 Torr. ALICE (Deisting) data are at 1 atm but < 5 Td.

### Cited Findings

**Coimbra (LIP), low pressure (6–12 Torr), 10–45 Td, GEM/CsI photoelectron source with ion drift time-of-flight**
- Pure Ar at 30 Td, 7 Torr: K0 = 1.57 (Ar+) and 1.92 (Ar2+) — [Santos et al. 2018](https://indico.cern.ch/event/709670/contributions/3019642/attachments/1670094/2678865/Ion_Mobility_studies_in_Ar-CF4-iC4H10_mixtures_for_the_LCTPC_Collaboration.pdf). The original reference is Neves et al., J. Chem. Phys. 133, 124316 (2010), on atomic and dimer Ar, Kr and Xe ions — [cited in Cortez 2020](https://indico.global/event/6159/contributions/50740/attachments/25481/43821/RD51_Presentation_-_Ion_mobility.pdf)
- Pure Xe at 15 Td, 8 Torr: K0 ≈ 0.578 (Xe+) and 0.642 (Xe2+). Resonant charge transfer Xe+ + Xe has k = 2.5×10⁻¹⁰ cm³/s and "delays the atomic ions" — [Cortez 2020](https://indico.global/event/6159/contributions/50740/attachments/25481/43821/RD51_Presentation_-_Ion_mobility.pdf)
- Pure CF4 at 15 Td, 8 Torr: K0 ≈ 1.10–1.12 (CF3+), against a Langevin value of 1.13. A slower peak at about 0.96 is attributed to CF3+·CF4 (Langevin 0.92) — [Santos et al. 2017](https://indico.cern.ch/event/676702/contributions/2809870/attachments/1574392/2486508/MSantos_RD51_Miniweek_December2017.pdf)
- Ar-CF4, 97% Ar: K(CF3+) ≈ 2.27. Behaviour is described by Blanc's law and Langevin theory — [Santos et al. 2018](https://indico.cern.ch/event/709670/contributions/3019642/attachments/1670094/2678865/Ion_Mobility_studies_in_Ar-CF4-iC4H10_mixtures_for_the_LCTPC_Collaboration.pdf)
- Pure iC4H10 at 15 Td: K0(C8Hn+) ≈ 0.65 and K0(C12Hn+) ≈ 0.60 — [Santos et al. 2018](https://indico.cern.ch/event/709670/contributions/3019642/attachments/1670094/2678865/Ion_Mobility_studies_in_Ar-CF4-iC4H10_mixtures_for_the_LCTPC_Collaboration.pdf)
- 98/2 Ar/iC4H10 at 20 Td, 8 Torr: K0(C4Hn+) ≈ 2.26 and K0(C8Hn+) ≈ 1.81 (extrapolated). Blanc's law holds across 0–100% Ar — [Santos et al. 2018](https://indico.cern.ch/event/709670/contributions/3019642/attachments/1670094/2678865/Ion_Mobility_studies_in_Ar-CF4-iC4H10_mixtures_for_the_LCTPC_Collaboration.pdf)
- 60/40 CF4/iC4H10 at 20 Td: K0(C8Hn+) ≈ 0.67 — [Santos et al. 2018](https://indico.cern.ch/event/709670/contributions/3019642/attachments/1670094/2678865/Ion_Mobility_studies_in_Ar-CF4-iC4H10_mixtures_for_the_LCTPC_Collaboration.pdf)
- Ar/CF4/iC4H10 95/3/2 at 8 Torr: K0(C4Hn+) = 2.16 ± 0.02, against 2.15 from Blanc/Langevin — [Santos et al. 2018](https://indico.cern.ch/event/709670/contributions/3019642/attachments/1670094/2678865/Ion_Mobility_studies_in_Ar-CF4-iC4H10_mixtures_for_the_LCTPC_Collaboration.pdf)
- Ar/CF4/CH4 92/3/5 at 15 Td, 8 Torr shows three peaks, K0 = 2.95 ± 0.02, 2.55 ± 0.02 and 2.19 ± 0.01. The expected values are 3.12 for C2Hn+, 2.40 for C3Hn+ and 2.15 for CF3+ — [Santos et al. 2017](https://indico.cern.ch/event/676702/contributions/2809870/attachments/1574392/2486508/MSantos_RD51_Miniweek_December2017.pdf)
- Pure CH4 (Trindade et al. 2012): zero-field K0 = 2.58 (CH5+) and 2.42 (C2H5+/C3H7+) — [ResearchGate: Experimental measurements of the mobility of methane ions in methane (snippet)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/254496443_Experimental_measurements_of_the_mobility_of_methane_ions_in_methane)
- Ar-C2H6: Cortez et al., "Experimental measurements of the mobility of methane ions in Ar-C2H6", JINST 8 P12012 (2013). The paper is cited, but its values were not retrieved — [search summary](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1748-0221/11/05/P05005/pdf)
- Ne-CO2 and CO2-N2 (Encarnação et al., JINST 11 P05005, 2016) were measured at 8–12 Torr and 10–25 Td. Only one peak appears for > 25% CO2, attributed to a CO2-originated ion. The main-peak K0 runs from about 2.11 ± 0.04 to 1.10 ± 0.03 over 1–99% CO2 — [CDS full text](https://cds.cern.ch/record/2255747/files/Encarna%C3%A7%C3%A3o_2016_J._Inst._11_P05005.pdf) (values from search snippet)
- Ar-CO2: Encarnação et al., JINST 10 P01010 (2015). One reference list gives it as JINST 9 P07008 — [Cortez 2020 references](https://indico.global/event/6159/contributions/50740/attachments/25481/43821/RD51_Presentation_-_Ion_mobility.pdf). Its values were not retrieved directly.
- Other Coimbra mixtures exist, but their values were not retrieved here: Ar-N2 (JINST 13 P11016, 2018, 10–25 Td) — [IOP](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1748-0221/13/11/P11016); Xe-CO2 (JINST 12 P06012, 2017); Xe-C2H6 — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/320512482_Experimental_ion_mobility_measurements_in_Xe-C_2_H_6); and Ne-CF4 (RD51 Dec 2018, D.J.G. **ALICE / GSI (Deisting, Garabatos, Szabo, Vranic): atmospheric pressure, very low field**
- The measurements were made with a GEM-stack ion source, drift to a wire grid, E_drift up to 1100 V/cm (0.84–4.6 Td), p = 966 ± 9 mbar and T = 293.5 K. Blanc-law extrapolation of Ar-CO2 gives K0 = 1.94 ± 0.01 for the (cluster) ion in pure Ar and 1.10 ± 0.01 in pure CO2. Ne-CO2 gives 4.06 ± 0.07 in pure Ne and 1.09 ± 0.01 in pure CO2. For the ALICE TPC baseline Ne-CO2-N2 (90-10-5), K0 = 2.92 ± 0.04 at 130 ppm H2O. Adding several hundred ppm of water slightly decreases K0 — [Deisting et al., NIM A 904 (2018), arXiv:1804.10288 full text](https://tpc_gas.cfnssbu.physics.sunysb.edu/tpc_gas/Papers/1804_10288.pdf)
- The paper notes that clustering depends on p and T, "which makes an extrapolation between measurements at different T and p not significant". This is a direct caveat against transferring Coimbra's 8 Torr numbers to 1 atm — [Deisting et al. 2018](https://tpc_gas.cfnssbu.physics.sunysb.edu/tpc_gas/Papers/1804_10288.pdf)
- A 2016 RD51 comparison found the GSI Ar-CO2 results lay below Coimbra's, attributed to differences in water content and pressure — [Deisting RD51 March 2016](https://indico.cern.ch/event/496113/contributions/2008316/attachments/1241335/1826982/RD51-March-2016.pdf) (via search summary)

### Inferences
- **Langevin checks for the Ar/iC4H10 ions.** K0 = 13.88/√(αμ), with α(Ar) = 1.64 Å³ and μ in u. The results are:

  | Ion | μ (u) | Langevin K0 | Measured |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | C3H7+ (43 u) in Ar | 20.7 | ≈ 2.38 | — |
  | t-C4H9+ (57 u) in Ar | 23.5 | ≈ 2.24 | 2.26 |
  | C8H17+ (113 u) in Ar | 29.5 | ≈ 1.99 | 1.81 (extrapolated) |
  | Ar2+ in Ar | 26.7 | ≈ 2.10 | 1.92 |
  | Ar+ in Ar | 20 | ≈ 2.42 | 1.53 |

  The 37% shortfall for Ar+ is the signature of resonant charge exchange (Q3).
- For 95/5 Ar/iC4H10 at low field, Blanc's law with C4H9+ gives 1/K0 = 0.95/2.24 + 0.05/K(C4H9+ in iC4H10). Coimbra measured C8 in iC4H10 at 0.65. For C4H9+ in iC4H10, Langevin with α ≈ 8.1 Å³ and μ ≈ 28.7 u gives about 0.91, so K0_mix ≈ 2.1–2.2.

### Gaps
- None of the retrieved Coimbra data go above 45 Td. The Coimbra pages and slides did not report a measurement for any hydrocarbon ion in Ar above about 45 Td.
- K0 tables for the Ar-CO2 (2015), Ar-CH4 (2014) and Ar-C2H6 (2013) papers were not retrieved.
- No 2021–2026 Coimbra/DRD1 ion-mobility papers were found by the searches. This may be a search limitation, so INSPIRE should be checked.

## Q3. Mobility at high E/N (~100–150 Td, the Micromegas amplification gap); Ar+ in Ar and resonant charge exchange; Blanc/Langevin validity

### Takeaway
Only Ar+ in Ar (and a few atomic or parent-gas ions) has measured high-E/N mobility. K0 falls from 1.53 at low field to about 1.21 at 124 Td and 0.56 at 1000 Td, because resonant charge exchange dominates. **No measurements exist for C3H7+, C4H9+ or other hydrocarbon fragment ions in Ar at ~100–150 Td.** Theory says that, for a non-resonant ion in Ar, K0 at 124 Td should stay within a few tens of % of its low-field (Langevin-like) value of about 2.2–2.4. It should not collapse the way Ar+ does. Heating at high E/N should also break up weakly bound clusters (CO2+·CO2), so the cluster ions measured at low field may not be the ions present in the gap.

### Cited Findings
- The Garfield++ `Data/IonMobility_Ar+_Ar.txt` table (T = 300 K; accuracy 1% below 250 Td, 3% above) is built from Hornbeck (1951), Beaty (1968) up to 2000 Td, and Basurto et al. (Phys. Rev. E 61, 3053, 2000). Selected points (E/N in Td: K0): 0: 1.53; 20: 1.51; 50: 1.41; 80: 1.32; 100: 1.27; 120: 1.22; 150: 1.16; 200: 1.06; 300: 0.95; 500: 0.78; 1000: 0.56; 2000: 0.40; 9000: 0.19. That interpolates to **K0(Ar+/Ar) ≈ 1.21 at 124 Td** — [Garfield++ Data directory, gitlab master (fetched 2026-10-15)](https://gitlab.cern.ch/garfield/garfieldpp/-/tree/master/Data)
- Hegerberg, Elford and Skullerud measured Ar+ mobility in Ar at 293 K up to 1000 Td. They derived the symmetric charge-exchange cross section for about 0.11–0.63 eV lab energy to better than ±5% — [J. Phys. B 15, 797 (1982)](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/0022-3700/15/5/022) (via search summary)
- Guided-ion-beam Ar+ + Ar symmetric charge-transfer cross sections (0.2–300 eV) lie significantly above the Rapp–Francis model. A corrected one-electron model is proposed — [Pullins et al., OSTI record](https://www.osti.gov/etdeweb/biblio/20144590)
- Phelps (J. Appl. Phys. 1994) gives a consistent set of Ar+ + Ar elastic plus charge-transfer differential and integral cross sections for sheath and Monte Carlo modelling, treating symmetric charge transfer as part of elastic (backward) scattering — [summary of Phelps 1994](https://ouci.dntb.gov.ua/en/works/4bnDgDa4/) (via search summary)
- Ristivojević and Petrović (PSST 21, 035001, 2012) built a Monte Carlo method for ion transport with thermal background-gas motion, benchmarked on Ar+ in Ar — [IOP](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/0963-0252/21/3/035001)
- Other Garfield++ tables over the same range (E/N in Td: K0): CO2+ in CO2 from Viehland & Mason (ADNDT 60, 37, 1995) at 303 K, 4% accuracy: 50: 1.26; 100: 1.21; 120: 1.19; 200: 1.08. CF4+ in CF4 (H. Ishiura) rises with field: 30: 0.944; 60: 0.924; 100: 1.026; 150: 1.171; 200: 1.206 — [Garfield++ Data](https://gitlab.cern.ch/garfield/garfieldpp/-/tree/master/Data)
- Resonant charge transfer is listed explicitly as a process that "slows down ions" (Xe+ + Xe, k = 2.5×10⁻¹⁰ cm³/s, "delays the atomic ions"). The same source states that **"High E/N favour declustering"** (ion heating, dissociation of hydrates; citing Eiceman et al. 2013), and that low E/N favours water clusters — [Cortez 2020](https://indico.global/event/6159/contributions/50740/attachments/25481/43821/RD51_Presentation_-_Ion_mobility.pdf)
- Langevin theory is expected to fail for "large molecules" and "weakly polarizable atoms/molecules". Cortez proposes empirical mass–mobility fits (Kalkan 2015 data) as a better estimator — [Cortez 2020](https://indico.global/event/6159/contributions/50740/attachments/25481/43821/RD51_Presentation_-_Ion_mobility.pdf)
- Blanc's law 1/K_mix = Σ f_i/K_i describes Coimbra's Ar-CF4, Ar-iC4H10 and CF4-iC4H10 data "well", together with Langevin theory — [Santos et al. 2018](https://indico.cern.ch/event/709670/contributions/3019642/attachments/1670094/2678865/Ion_Mobility_studies_in_Ar-CF4-iC4H10_mixtures_for_the_LCTPC_Collaboration.pdf). Deisting used Blanc fits to extract the pure-gas limits — [Deisting et al. 2018](https://tpc_gas.cfnssbu.physics.sunysb.edu/tpc_gas/Papers/1804_10288.pdf)
- At low pressure the reduced mobility depends only on E/N. At higher pressure it can also depend on pressure at high E/N (MD study of polyatomic ions in He) — [search summary](https://ogarev-online.ru/2308-1120/article/view/135343)

### Inferences
- **Why Ar+ is slow.** In Ar+ + Ar, resonant charge transfer has a cross section of tens of Å² that is nearly flat with energy. A transferred charge restarts from a thermal neutral, so it acts like a large backward-scattering momentum-transfer cross section. This gives the low-field K0 of 1.53, against a polarization limit of about 2.4. Because σ_ct is roughly constant, v_d grows like √E at high field (mean-free-path-limited regime), so K ∝ (E/N)^−½. The table follows this: 0.56 at 1000 Td and 0.19 at 9000 Td. **Using the Ar+ table for the Micromegas ion tail in Ar/iC4H10 is therefore physically wrong.** The real ion (C4H9+ or C3H7+) has no resonant channel with Ar.
- **Wannier estimate at 124 Td for C4H9+ in Ar, 1 atm.** E ≈ 33 kV/cm at N0. With K0 ≈ 2.2, v_d ≈ 7.3×10⁴ cm/s. The Wannier effective temperature is T_eff = T + M_gas·v_d²/(3k) ≈ 293 + 860 ≈ 1150 K (mean relative energy ≈ 0.15 eV). At these energies the ion–Ar interaction probes the repulsive wall as well as the r⁻⁴ polarization tail. Viehland–Mason two-temperature theory typically gives K changing by roughly ±10–30% for polyatomic ions in Ar at T_eff ≈ 1000 K. **The expected K0 for C4H9+ in Ar at 124 Td is therefore ≈ 1.8–2.5 cm²/Vs, best guess about 2.1–2.2.** This is unmeasured and must be flagged as an extrapolation. It is about 1.8× faster than the Ar+ table value (1.21), so a simulated Micromegas ion signal would be about 1.8× shorter.
- **Gap transit time.** In a 128 µm gap at ~124 Td, an ion with K0 = 2.2 crosses in about 175 ns, while one with K0 = 1.21 takes about 320 ns. Most of the ion-induced charge in a Micromegas is collected in the gap, so the ion-tail duration scales directly with 1/K.
- **Blanc's law at high E/N.** Blanc's law is exact only when the ion velocity distribution is the same in each component gas. At high E/N, T_eff differs between components (it depends on the gas mass), so Blanc's law should be applied at a matched mean ion energy, not at the same E/N (the Mason–Hahn correction). For 95/5 Ar/iC4H10 the iC4H10 term contributes only about 10% of 1/K, so this correction is small.
- **CO2 clusters at high field.** CO2+·CO2 binding is about 0.6 eV (Hiraoka, cited in Cortez 2020; value not retrieved). With T_eff ≈ 1000 K plus collisional heating, partial declustering in a Micromegas gap is plausible. The gap ion could therefore be CO2+ rather than the slower cluster. This is speculative.

### Gaps
- **No measured mobility data were found for any hydrocarbon fragment ion (C3H7+, C3H5+, C4H9+, C4H10+) in Ar above about 45 Td.** Searches of Coimbra papers, Garfield++ data and LXCat summaries all came up empty.
- Ellis et al. ADNDT compilations and the LXCat Viehland database were not accessed directly. It remains to check whether they contain alkyl ions in Ar at high E/N (unlikely; they mostly hold atomic ions and small molecular ions such as NO+, O2+ and N2+).
- Phelps' Ar+/Ar high-E/N mobility data were not fetched directly. The Garfield++ table (Hornbeck/Beaty/Basurto) is used instead.
- No ion–Ar interaction potential for C4H9+ was found. With one, a GC/MOBCAL-style or Monte Carlo calculation could give K(E/N) from first principles.

## Q4. What simulation tools do with ion species and mobility

### Takeaway
Garfield++ treats ions as a single species per medium. You load one K0(E/N) table, or set a constant, and that is used for all ions everywhere. Ion identity never changes during transport, and no charge transfer or clustering is modelled. Blanc's law is not applied automatically. The shipped tables are Ar+/Ar, CO2+/CO2, CF4+/CF4, He+/He, Ne+/Ne, Xe+/Xe (two fine-structure states), SF6−/SF6 and, since the Coimbra work, C8Hn+/iC4H10. No mixture table is shipped. Users of Ar/iC4H10 commonly load the Ar+ table, which is the wrong ion and the wrong high-field behaviour. Full ion-chemistry transport exists only in plasma/IMS codes (Monte Carlo swarm codes), not in detector simulation.

### Cited Findings
- The Garfield++ `Data/` directory on master (fetched 2026-10-15) contains: `IonMobility_Ar+_Ar.txt`, `IonMobility_C8Hn+_iC4H10.txt`, `IonMobility_CF4+_CF4.txt`, `IonMobility_CO2+_CO2.txt`, `IonMobility_He+_He.txt`, `IonMobility_Ne+_Ne.txt`, `IonMobility_SF6-_SF6.txt`, `IonMobility_Xe+_P12_Xe.txt` and `IonMobility_Xe+_P32_Xe.txt`, plus `Deexcitation` data and other unrelated tables — [Garfield++ gitlab Data/](https://gitlab.cern.ch/garfield/garfieldpp/-/tree/master/Data)
- `IonMobility_C8Hn+_iC4H10.txt` is a flat K0 = 0.65 over 0–45 Td (T = 293 K), citing Cortez et al. JINST 14 P04010. It contains no data above 45 Td — [Garfield++ Data](https://gitlab.cern.ch/garfield/garfieldpp/-/tree/master/Data)
- `MediumGas::LoadIonMobility(filename)` and `LoadNegativeIonMobility(filename)` both call `LoadMobility`. That function reads two columns (E/N in Td, K0 in cm²/Vs) and looks in `$GARFIELD_INSTALL/share/Garfield/Data/` if needed. It scales by the gas table's pressure and temperature and calls `SetIonMobility(efields, mobilities, negative)`. There is one table per medium, with no per-component or per-species structure — [Garfield++ Source/MediumGas.cc](https://gitlab.cern.ch/garfield/garfieldpp/-/blob/master/Source/MediumGas.cc)
- `Medium::IonMobility()` returns `m_iMob[0][0][0]`, a single table. `NegativeIonMobility()` falls back to the positive-ion table if none is set. Medium also has an `IonDissociation` coefficient with its own extrapolation and interpolation settings. That is a rate on the single ion population, not a species change — [Garfield++ Source/Medium.cc](https://gitlab.cern.ch/garfield/garfieldpp/-/blob/master/Source/Medium.cc)
- A ROOT-forum answer says the ion-mobility files cover "a few gases" and come from literature compilations. Another notes that the Garfield++ files and the papers they cite don't always match — [ROOT forum: How to get the Ion Mobility file](https://root-forum.cern.ch/t/how-to-get-the-ion-mobility-file/48339); [ROOT forum: Ion mobility of Isobutane in Garfield++](https://root-forum.cern.ch/t/ion-mobility-of-isobutane-in-garfield/44446); [ROOT forum: What is needed in a mobility file](https://root-forum.cern.ch/t/what-is-needed-in-a-mobility-file/42099); [ROOT forum: Ion mobility file in He-isobutane mixture](https://root-forum.cern.ch/t/ion-mobility-file-in-he-isobutane-mixture/56843)
- Ions cannot be tracked microscopically in Garfield++, unlike electrons. Ion transport uses drift-line (AvalancheMC/DriftLineRKF) methods with the tabulated mobility — [Misbah, MSc thesis, Frankfurt 2021](https://www.uni-frankfurt.de/103445398/MA_Misbah_2021.pdf)
- In Garfield++ the Micromegas ion tail is enabled via the ion-signal option, and every avalanche ion is drifted with the loaded mobility — [ROOT forum: Micromegas signal calculation](https://root-forum.cern.ch/t/signal-calculation-of-low-energy-particles-and-fission-fragments-in-a-micromegas-geometry/45235). Ion-backflow Micromegas studies use the same machinery — [aglaenze/COMSOL Garfield IBF project](https://github.com/aglaenze/COMSOL)
- Ion Monte Carlo codes with full collision physics exist in the plasma community: Ristivojević & Petrović 2012 (thermal background), and Phelps-type Ar+/Ar cross sections used in sheath MC. These are demonstrated on Ar+/Ar, not on detector-gas chemistry — [Ristivojević & Petrović, PSST 21 035001](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/0963-0252/21/3/035001); [Phelps-type MC example, arXiv:1605.08587](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1605.08587)
- LXCat (www.lxcat.net) hosts swarm and transport parameters and ion–neutral interaction potentials, with contributor-owned databases. Viehland's long-standing gaseous-ion transport database became the web-based ICEcat around 2010 (LAPLACE) — [LXCat platform paper](https://oa.tib.eu/renate/items/806f2d1d-6382-4534-a2d6-25d9e5dd51b5/full); [ICEcat GEC 2011 abstract](https://meetings-archive.aps.org/gec/2011/gt1/4)

### Inferences
- Simulating an Ar/iC4H10 (or Ar/CF4/iC4H10) Micromegas properly in Garfield++ means writing a custom two-column table for the actual drifting ion (C4H9+ in the mixture) and loading it with `LoadIonMobility`. At low field, use the Blanc's-law value with Coimbra's measured K0 ≈ 2.2–2.26, extended to about 150 Td with a flat or mildly varying K0. State explicitly that the high-field part is an extrapolation. Garfield++ cannot represent a time-dependent species change (Ar+ → C3H7+ → C4H9+ → C8H17+), but at 1 atm the first two steps happen in ≲ 1 ns and the third takes ≫ 1 µs, so a single species (C4H9+) is a good approximation for the Micromegas gap.
- The bundled C8Hn+/iC4H10 table describes pure-iC4H10 condensation products at 8 Torr. It is not appropriate for Ar-rich mixtures at 1 atm.
- Garfield++ extrapolates outside the table range (configurable via `SetExtrapolationMethodIonMobility`; the default behaviour was not checked here). A table ending at 45 Td is therefore silently extrapolated to Micromegas-gap fields.

### Gaps
- No detector-simulation code was found that tracks ion species changes during drift. None was found in the searches (Garfield++, its forum, and the Micromegas/IBF simulation repositories); this absence is not proven.
- No published Micromegas comparison was found in which switching the ion species (e.g. Ar+ vs hydrocarbon ion mobility) was shown to change simulated signal or ion-tail results against data. Kalkan et al. 2015 make this argument qualitatively for Ar-CO2 and Ne-CO2 (cluster ions alter the signals) — [JINST 10 P07004](https://acikerisim.uludag.edu.tr/bitstreams/a45ce400-f4f3-4d2b-9b8c-e7c78bc875a9/download). R. Veenhof's talks "Which ions are producing the signals?" (RD51 June 2020) and "Cluster ions" (RD51 Dec 2014) are cited in [Cortez 2020](https://indico.global/event/6159/contributions/50740/attachments/25481/43821/RD51_Presentation_-_Ion_mobility.pdf) but were not retrieved.
- Coimbra's own "IonMobility" analysis tools, Viehland's GC/MOBCAL-type codes, and the actual contents of LXCat's ion section for detector gases were not examined in this session.
- Space-charge-induced changes in drift velocity with gain were observed in µTPC-MIMAC Garfield++ vs data comparisons — [arXiv:2003.11812](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.11812). Separating this from ion-species effects was not attempted in the literature found.
